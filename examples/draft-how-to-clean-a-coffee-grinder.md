# How to Clean a Coffee Grinder (Without Losing Your Grind Setting)

Empty the beans, run it for a few seconds to clear the burrs, unplug it, then take off the hopper and the top burr and brush everything out. Keep water away from the burrs. Brush it out once a week, and do a full clean about once a month (more often if you grind dark, oily beans). Don't put rice through a burr grinder.

Before you pull anything apart, write down your grind setting or take a photo of it. Breville, Fellow and the OXO manual don't mention it, and it saves you dialing in from scratch.

Why bother? The oils and coffee particles left in the grinder ["can grow stale, even rancid,"](https://www.breville.com/us/en/blog/coffee-and-espresso/how-to-clean-a-coffee-grinder.html) and your fresh beans go through all of it.

[Fellow's page](https://fellowproducts.com/blogs/learn/how-to-clean-a-coffee-grinder) says monthly in one place and 1 to 3 months in another. Its table is the useful part:

- Occasional use: every 2 to 3 months.
- Daily drinker: every 1 to 2 months.
- Espresso, or dark and oily beans: once or twice a month.
- Stalling or an off taste: clean it right away.

Fellow and Breville both say dark roasts mean cleaning more often. Breville adds that burr grinders "extract more oils" than blade grinders.

## What you need

The brush that came with your grinder (or an old toothbrush), a microfiber cloth, a dry towel, and a small vacuum or silicone blower if you have one. Fellow says to keep metal brushes and abrasive scourers off the burrs.

## How to deep clean a burr grinder, step by step

These steps follow the [OXO Brew Conical Burr manual](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/OXO-Brew-Conical-Burr-Coffee-Grinder-8717000-UserManual.pdf), since it's a common first grinder. Your grinder may twist and release differently, so keep your own manual open.

### 1. Write down your grind setting

Take a photo of the dial or write the number on a sticky note. If your grinder is stepless or has a collar, mark where it sits.

You'll need it in step 7.

[ADD: photo of your setting before you start]

### 2. Empty the hopper, run it empty, then unplug it

Pour out the beans, then run the grinder for about 10 seconds with the hopper empty to clear what's left in the burrs. Then unplug it.

### 3. Take off the hopper and the top burr

On the OXO, turn the hopper counterclockwise until it clicks and lift it off. A trap door keeps any beans inside. Then lift the metal handle on the upper burr, twist it counterclockwise and pull it up.

On the OXO, only the upper burr is removable. Other grinders differ, so check your manual before you try to get at the lower burr.

### 4. Brush the burrs, the chamber and the chute

Use a small bristle brush on both burrs, the grinding chamber and the grounds chute. OXO says never to use water or any other liquid on the inside of the grinder or the burrs.

Breville is looser: it allows a damp cloth on the burrs, but says never soak them or machine-wash them, and dry them completely because they rust. If your manual says no liquid, as OXO's does, follow the manual.

### 5. Wash only what the manual says is washable

For the OXO, that means the hopper and the grounds container. Wash them by hand, rinse, and dry them thoroughly. The scoop can go in the dishwasher. Nothing else can, and no abrasive pads, harsh chemicals or spray cleaners either. Wipe the outside of the grinder with a soft, damp cloth.

### 6. Put the burr back and check it's seated

Line up the indents on the burr with the tabs, turn it clockwise until it clicks, then pull the handle to test it. Beans left around the burrs can stop it from locking, so give the area one more brush first.

### 7. Put the hopper back, reset your setting and purge

Line up the hopper's tabs with the notches, press down and turn clockwise until it locks. Set it back to the number you wrote down (more on that below).

Then grind a small amount of fresh beans to purge the grinder and throw those grounds away. Breville calls this "seasoning" the burrs. If you used cleaning tablets, this step isn't optional: Urnex's last step is to run coffee beans through after them.

## Getting your grind setting back after cleaning

On the OXO, the grind setting is the hopper itself. You rotate the hopper to change the grind, from 1 (fine) to 15 (coarse), and you just took it off. So once it locks back in, check the number and turn it back to yours. If there are beans in it, turn it with the grinder running so it doesn't jam.

As a quick check, OXO's own ranges are:

- Espresso: 1 to 5
- Pour over and drip: 6 to 10
- French press and cold brew: 11 to 15

Then grind a test dose and compare it to what you normally get. For filter coffee, the [grind size chart](/grind-size-chart) shows you what you're aiming for. For espresso, a small shift matters a lot more, so go through [how to dial in espresso](/espresso-dialing-in).

## How to clean a blade grinder

Blade grinders are simpler. Breville's four steps:

1. Unplug it.
2. Brush out the grounds, or use compressed air.
3. Wipe the blades and the chamber with a damp cloth.
4. Dry it fully.

This is the one place rice is fine: Breville says a minute of rice in a blade grinder. If your model has a removable bowl, wash it only if the manual says you can.

If you're still on a blade grinder and your coffee comes out uneven, [our burr grinder guide](/best-burr-grinders-under-200) covers the upgrade.

## Model-specific steps

### OXO Brew Conical Burr

Run it empty for 10 seconds and unplug it. Turn the hopper counterclockwise to lift it off, then twist out the upper burr by its handle. Brush the burrs and chute (no water), hand-wash only the hopper and grounds container, and wipe the body with a damp cloth. Put the burr and hopper back, then turn the hopper to your number. ## Signs your grinder needs a clean now

Breville's list:

- Visible residue or oil
- Off-color grinds after switching beans
- Unusual noises or slower grinding
- Difficulty adjusting the grind setting

OXO adds one: dark, oily beans can clog the grounds chute. Brush the chute, and if it keeps happening, use cleaning tablets.

## Mistakes that wreck grinders

- **Water on the burrs.** Fellow says no water near the burrs, motor or internal parts unless the manufacturer says it's safe.
- **The dishwasher.** On the OXO, only the scoop goes in.
- **Abrasive pads, harsh chemicals and spray cleaners.** OXO's manual rules out all three.
- **Reassembling while damp.** Dry everything first. The parts rust.
- **Rice in a burr grinder.** See above.
- **Compressed air on a burr grinder.** Mahlkönig says it ["can force debris deeper into grinding mechanisms and can damage sensitive internal components"](https://www.mahlkoenig.us/blogs/news/clean-commercial-coffee-grinders) and recommends a vacuum instead. On a burr grinder, use a brush or a vacuum.

## FAQ

**Should I clean a brand-new grinder?**
Yes, a quick one. OXO's manual says each unit "was tested to guarantee quality," so "a small amount of coffee grounds may remain inside," and tells you to clean and wash all parts before first use. Fellow goes further and suggests "purging as much coffee as possible, ideally up to 1 lb," which also seasons the burrs.
